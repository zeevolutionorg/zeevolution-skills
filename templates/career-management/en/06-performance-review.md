> 💡 **Writing rules:** second person ("you"), direct, specific. Every claim has evidence: a link, a number, a name. Praise is as specific as criticism. No softeners on gaps.

## 🎯 Team goals for {{cycle}}

- **[+]** [G1] 
- **[=]** [G2] 
- **[-]** [G3] 

> 💡 **How to use:** list the team's goals for the cycle and mark each one: **[+]** achieved, **[=]** partly achieved, **[-]** missed or moved. If the team missed for reasons outside the person's control, say so in one line: context is fairness.
>
> ✍️ **Example:** "[-] [G2] Self-serve onboarding live for small businesses — moved to next cycle for team-level reasons; you joined when the team was at about half capacity."

## 🚀 Impact on these goals

- **[G1]** 
- **[G2]** 
- **[G1 — at what cost]** 

> 💡 **How to use:** one bullet per delivery, tagged with the goal it served. Say what you did with an authorship verb (*sole author of*, *main author of*, *led*, *built under X's direction*) and the result with a number. End with one **"at what cost"** bullet: the hidden price of the delivery (rework, review load, late scope decisions) — that's usually where the growth is.
>
> ✍️ **Example (Engineering):** "[G1] Main author of the payments retry service: 12 pull requests across two services; failed payments recovered went from 41% to 63%."
>
> ✍️ **Example (Design):** "[G2] Sole author of the new onboarding flow; adopted by two teams outside the squad within a month."
>
> ✍️ **Example (at what cost):** "[G1 — at what cost] Requirements were settled in code rather than before it; three rounds of rework and about 20 open review threads at the end."

## 📣 Main message

> 💡 **How to use:** one paragraph, in this order: (1) what you did — **strengths first; that needs to be said first**; (2) a **bold one-line verdict on level** (e.g. "**You are a solid {{level}}.**" / "**You are operating at {{level}} and heading toward {{next_level}}.**" / "**This cycle was below what {{level}} expects.**"); (3) the gap; (4) **the three moves** for next cycle; (5) **the arena** — the real upcoming work where they'll show up; (6) anything "alongside" (update the development plan, keep the Brag current).
>
> ✍️ **Example:** "You took the forecasting rebuild from a spreadsheet to a model that operations now plans with every week — that needs to be said first. **You are a solid Contributing data scientist, heading toward Owning.** What separates the two is turning insight into decisions others act on. Next cycle the three moves are: (1) one decision memo a month that a PM acts on; (2) close every open question in your analyses with an owner and a date; (3) present your own readouts to the product director. The arena is the pricing experiments in Q2. Alongside: refresh your Growth Plan with these moves and keep the Brag current every two weeks."
